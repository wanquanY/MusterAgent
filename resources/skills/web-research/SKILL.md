# Web Research

## Goal and scope

Produce an evidence-backed answer from relevant external sources while clearly separating sourced facts, synthesis, and inference. This Skill does not authorize interactive browser control or authenticated account actions.

## Inputs and prerequisites

- Identify the exact question, freshness requirement, geography, date range, and acceptable source classes.
- Prefer primary documentation, official data, first-party announcements, and original research when available.
- Use more than one independent source when the claim is disputed, high impact, or likely to have changed.

## Workflow

1. Search with targeted queries that cover the main claim and plausible counter-evidence.
2. Open the most authoritative results and verify that the page itself supports the intended claim.
3. Record dates, scope limitations, methodology, and any discrepancy between publication date and event date.
4. Synthesize without copying long passages; link citations next to the claims they support.
5. Mark conclusions derived from combining sources as inference.

## Failure and stop rules

- Do not cite a search result snippet as if the source page had been verified.
- Stop or qualify the answer when sources are inaccessible, stale, circular, or materially contradictory.
- Do not invent links, quotations, authors, dates, metrics, or consensus.

## Completion and verification

Every time-sensitive or externally verifiable material claim must have a directly supporting source, and the final answer must expose uncertainty and unresolved conflicts. Search execution alone is not evidence that the answer is correct.
